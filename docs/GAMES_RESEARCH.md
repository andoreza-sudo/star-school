# Games research: what children play most, and three new games for the Game Room

Chunk G-03, written 2026-11-11 by Claude. For Andy (the owner) to pick from. The picked games are built in G-04, one chunk each.

This page has five parts:

1. What I looked at, and what I could not look at.
2. What children aged 5 to 11 play most right now.
3. Which game ideas are good for children, and which to keep out.
4. Three new games for Star School, one page each.
5. My ranking and recommendation.

The sources are listed at the end, with dates.

## 1. What I looked at

- Reports on what children play: the US games trade body (ESA, 2025), a US survey of parents of 3 to 11 year olds
  (Morning Consult, August 2025), a 2025 children's study by the kids' research agency KB&B, an Australian survey of
  6 to 13 year olds (Roy Morgan, December 2025), the UK media regulator's yearly report on children (Ofcom, May 2025) and a
  BCG games report (2025-2026).
- Download charts: the most downloaded mobile games of 2025 (Pocket Gamer.biz, Sensor Tower's monthly top 10 for October and
  December 2025, Games.gg's Google Play figures).
- Google Play's own lists: the Kids tab (all of it is "Teacher Approved"; it has age filters "up to 5", "6-8" and "9-12"),
  how Teacher Approved apps are judged, and Google Play's Best of 2025 awards.

- Research on what is healthy and what is not: a study of design tricks in apps for young children (JAMA Network Open, 2022),
  the UNICEF and LEGO study on digital play and children's well-being (April 2024), the classic study of why games pull us in
  (Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, 2006), the US regulator's case against loot boxes sold to children (FTC, January 2025) and the UK
  Children's Code guidance for game makers (ICO, 2023).

**R-02.** The roadmap says G-03 should be checked against R-02, the review of what brings children back to our own games. R-02

## 2. What children aged 5 to 11 play most

### 2.1 The big names

These names come up again and again in the reports and charts:

- **Roblox** and **Minecraft**: open "sandbox" worlds where children build, explore and play mini-games. Roblox was the
  second most downloaded mobile game of 2025 (286 million installs, Pocket Gamer.biz). Ofcom says it reaches about 6 in 10
  UK children aged 8 to 14 in a month. BCG found Minecraft, Super Mario and Roblox are the most common first games children
  ever play.
- **Block Blast**: a simple block-fitting puzzle. The most downloaded mobile game of 2025 (356 million installs).
- **Subway Surfers**: an endless runner. Still the most downloaded game on Google Play in 2025 by one count (Games.gg).
- **Pizza Ready!**: a "run a shop" game where you make and serve food and grow your restaurant. In Sensor Tower's top 5 by
  downloads in December 2025.
- **Toca Boca World**: a digital dollhouse for pretend play. Its maker says about 62 million children play it each month
  (Licensing Magazine, October 2025; this is the company's own figure).
- On the Kids tab and in expert lists: learning apps like Khan Academy Kids, PBS KIDS Games, ABCmouse (Google's "Best for
  Families" of 2025), Lingokids, Sago Mini World and LEGO and Bluey games. These are mostly for children under 8.
- Inside Roblox, the most played kinds of game are role-play (pretend towns and homes), "simulators" and "tycoons" (run and grow
  something), obstacle courses ("obbies"), and in 2025 a wave of grow-and-collect games. A gardening game and a collecting game
  together pushed Roblox to a record 47 million players at once on 23 August 2025. Fishing games were among the biggest new
  Roblox games of late 2025 (one tracker reports a fishing game passing 2.7 million players at once; I could not confirm that
  number elsewhere).

### 2.2 The genres, in plain words

| Genre | What you do | How big it is with 5-11 year olds | Already in Star School? |
|-------|-------------|-----------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Sandbox and building | Build and explore a world | Top genre for Gen Alpha in the US: half of parents say their child plays it (Morning Consult) | Yes, Block Builder (G-01 fixes it) |
| Adventure | Go somewhere, see new places, get to the end | Top genre in two 2025 surveys: 48% (KB&B), 43% of young Roblox players (Roy Morgan) | Not really |
| Action and arcade | Quick reactions: jump, dodge, catch, run | Arcade 67% and action 62% of Gen Alpha players (ESA); action 30-37% elsewhere; stronger with boys | Hill Climb (driving); G-02 racing |
| Puzzle | Fit, match and solve | 66% of Gen Alpha players (ESA); Block Blast tops downloads | Yes, Brain Teasers (17 puzzle types) |
| Simulation, role-play, "run a shop" | Pretend to be a cook, shopkeeper, vet, farmer; grow something | 28-32% (KB&B, Roy Morgan), especially popular with girls (KB&B); Pizza Ready!, Toca Boca World, Roblox role-play and tycoons | No |
| Creative | Draw, colour, make music, dress up | Strong in expert lists for 5-8 (Common Sense Media) | Yes, Coloring |
| Racing and sports | Drive fast, win the race | Boys more likely to prefer it (Morning Consult) | Hill Climb; G-02 racing |
| Virtual pet | Feed and care for a pet | Long-time chart regular | Yes, Pet Care and Pet Shop |
| Collecting | Fill an album or book | Built into most top games; a collecting card game was Google Play's Best Game of 2025 | Stickers only |

**What is missing from our Game Room:** a role-play / "run a shop" simulation, a skill-based action game that is not a
vehicle, and a calm game built around collecting. Those are the three gaps my proposals fill.

### 2.3 Why children like these games

The research gives a simple answer. Games pull us in when they make us feel three things (Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, 2006):

- **I am getting good at this** (competence). Controls that feel easy and natural, and challenge that grows with you.
- **I get to choose** (autonomy). Pick your path, your look, your way to play.
- **I am connected** (relatedness). For children this is often sharing: showing a parent or friend what they made or caught.
  The UNICEF study (2024) found that even one-player games are often social for children for this reason.

On top of that, children like:

- **Pretend play**: being a grown-up with a job (cook, shopkeeper, fisher, explorer).
- **Collecting**: a book with empty spaces that slowly fills up.
- **Making it mine**: colours, hats, decorations.
- **Surprise**: something new around the corner.
- **Quick wins**: a short round that ends with "Well done!".
- **Physical comedy**: bouncing, splashing, wobbling, things falling over (in a kind way).

## 3. Healthy game ideas, and ones to keep out

### 3.1 The problem

Most top children's games are free and earn money from ads or in-game purchases. That shapes their design. A 2022 study of the
apps that 3 to 5 year olds used most found that about 4 in 5 used design tricks to keep children playing or to get them to buy
(JAMA Network Open, Radesky and others). In January 2025 the US regulator fined the maker of a popular game 20 million dollars
for selling loot boxes to children in ways that hid the real cost (FTC). The UK Children's Code names "reward loops" like
streaks as a nudge to avoid with children.

Star School has no ads, no shop for real money and no network. So we can take the fun parts of these genres and leave the
tricks behind.

### 3.2 Keep: what makes children come back in a good way

| Mechanic | Why it is healthy | Used in |
|----------|-------------------|---------|
| Short rounds with a clear end | A natural place to stop. Fits our game time | All three |
| Skill you can see growing | Stars, medals, a personal best, getting faster | All three |
| A fixed set to collect | The book can be finished; no random paid draws | Fishing, Snack Stand menu |
| Make it yours | Decorate, choose colours and hats with points earned by playing | All three |
| Pretend play | Role-play builds language and imagination | Snack Stand, Fishing |
| Goals at three sizes | This round, this place or world, the whole game | All three |
| Fair, kind challenge | No game over, checkpoints, help after a few tries | All three |
| Two ways to play | Easy for a 5 year old, a stretch for a 10 year old, in the same game | All three |
| Gentle surprise | A rare fish, a surprise customer, a hidden star | All three |

### 3.3 Keep out: tricks to avoid

| Trick | What it looks like | Our rule |
|-------|--------------------|----------|
| Loot boxes and random prize draws | Pay (money or gems) for a mystery prize | Never. Every prize is earned by play and shown in advance |
| Energy and lives timers | "Wait 30 minutes or pay to play again" | Never. Game time is the only limit, and it is earned by learning |
| Ads | Videos between rounds, "watch an ad for a bonus" | Never (the app has none) |
| Streaks and daily login rewards | "Don't lose your 12-day streak!" | Not in these games |
| Limited-time offers | "Only today!", seasonal items that go away | Never |
| Comparing with others | Leaderboards, "your friend scored more" | Never. Children beat their own best |
| Endless loops | No end to the run, the next round starts by itself | Every round ends with the shared end card. No autoplay |
| "Your shop earned money while you were away" | Idle earnings that pull you back | Never. Nothing happens while the game is closed |
| Characters that guilt you | A character cries or begs when you leave | Never. Characters wave goodbye happily |
| Pay to win | Buy power instead of earning it | Never |
| Fake urgency | Flashing timers, "hurry!" | Only in optional modes for older children, and kind |

## 4. Three new games

What every one of them does (the house rules):

- It lives in the Game Room, is paid with game time like the others, and is registered with `GH.reg` (hub card, top bar,
  pause sheet and end card all come for free).
- A round lasts 3 to 10 minutes and ends with the shared end card (`GH.end`), or the shared "Game time's up!" card.
  Progress is saved first.
- It plays by touch on a cheap tablet, offline. Buttons are big (at least 48 px, main game buttons 64 px). It works on a phone
  (380 x 780) and a small tablet (1024 x 600).
- It is drawn in the app's own style (DESIGN_SYSTEM 6.5: soft, chunky drawings with light from the top left, colour tokens only,
  no emoji). Where it can, it reuses the 120 object drawings we already have.
- It has an easy way in for a 5 year old and depth for a 10 year old.
- It is one new file `src/js/NN-game-<name>.js` (plus an optional `NN-game-<name>.css`), which the build already puts in the
  games file. The games file is 230 KB today; the warning is at 400 KB, so there is room for G-02 and two or three more games.
- It is an original Star School game. It belongs to a genre, but it copies no real game's name, characters, art, levels or
  exact rules.

### Game A: Snack Stand

**Pitch:** Run your own little snack stand for one busy day: see what each animal customer wants, make it, and serve it with a
smile.

**Why children like this kind of game.** "Run a shop" and pretend-play games are some of the most played games for this age:
Pizza Ready! was one of the five most downloaded games in the world in December 2025 (Sensor Tower); Toca Boca World, a pretend
play world, has about 62 million players a month (company figure, 2025); role-play and "tycoon" games are among the most played
on Roblox. Surveys put simulation at about 3 in 10 children, and higher for girls (KB&B 2025, Roy Morgan 2025). Children love
playing at a grown-up job, and getting faster at it feels great.

**Core loop.**
1. An animal customer walks up to the counter. A speech bubble shows the order as pictures (for example: bread, cheese, bread;
   or a cone with two scoops; or an apple juice).
2. The child taps the ingredients on the counter to make it. The snack builds up on the plate as they tap.
3. They tap Serve (or drag the plate to the customer). The customer cheers, pays coins, and walks off happily.
4. The next customer comes. After 8 to 12 customers the sign turns to "Closed" and the day ends.

**Controls.**
- Tapping only. Big ingredient buttons in a row along the bottom.
- An Undo button takes the last ingredient off the plate.
- Older children: some foods need cooking. Tap to put toast or a pancake on the grill, tap again to take it off when it is
  golden (too early is pale, too late is a bit dark, never burnt or sad).

**Two ways to play.**
- **Calm** (the default for Pre-K to grade 1): one customer at a time, orders of 1 to 3 things, customers wait as long as you
  need.
- **Busy** (chosen by the child): two or three customers at once, longer orders, cooking, and a patience heart over each
  customer. If the heart runs out, the customer still gets the snack and smiles; you just miss the tip.

**Progression and how a round ends.**
- One round is one day (about 3 to 5 minutes). The end card shows stars (1 to 3, from how many orders were right first time),
  customers served, coins earned and the best "combo" (perfect orders in a row).
- Five places to open your stand, unlocked by stars: the park, the beach, a snowy hill, the fair and the moon. Each brings new
  foods and new animal customers.
- Coins buy things for the stand between days: new menu items, an awning colour, a sign, plants, a little helper. The list is
  fixed (about 30 things), all prices are shown, and everything can be bought by playing. When it is all bought, the stand is
  "complete" and the child can keep playing for stars.
- A small goal for each day ("Serve 3 smoothies") gives a reason to try a new place or food.

**What it looks like.** A front view of a wooden stall with a striped awning, the place's scene behind it (park trees, sea,
snow, fair lights, moon craters), drawn with our scene tokens. The animals (cat, dog, fox, duck, frog, bear, rabbit, owl,
sheep, cow, pig, lion, giraffe, elephant, mouse ...) and most foods (apple, banana, bread, cheese, egg, cookie, grapes, milk,
pizza, ice cream, cake, water) are already drawn in our object art. Only a few new pieces are needed: the stall, plate, cone,
cup, grill.

**Sound.** A soft till "ding" for coins, a sizzle on the grill, a happy chirp per animal, a little jingle when the sign turns
to Closed. All synthesised, like the rest of the app.

**How it stays healthy.**
- The day always ends. Nothing runs while the game is closed: no "your shop earned money while you were away".
- No angry or crying customers. Missing a tip is the only cost.
- Coins exist only inside the game and are earned only by playing it.
- The upgrade list has an end.

**Light learning tie-in (optional).** Each item has a price tag in coins. In a "Till" mode that a child can switch on, the
customer pays with a few coins and the child taps the right change from the till (for older children). Orders can also be shown
as words instead of pictures for children who read. Both are off by default; the game is fun without them.

**Risks.**
- Time pressure can stress young children: Calm is the default for the youngest, and Busy is never forced.
- Commercial games of this kind are often full of ads and wait timers; we take only the role-play and cooking.
- Picture orders must be easy to read at phone size: test at 380 x 780.

**Size.** Medium. About 35-45 KB of code with the stall and new food drawings, about 6 KB of CSS, and the hub picture. Most of
it can be built with HTML and SVG (no fast animation needed), which is gentle on cheap tablets.

### Game B: Sky Hop

**Pitch:** Bounce your pet through short obstacle courses in the clouds: jump, float and bounce to reach the flag, and find
the three hidden stars.

**Why children like this kind of game.** Adventure and action are the most played genres for this age in every survey I found
(adventure 43-48%, action 30-62%; KB&B 2025, Roy Morgan 2025, ESA 2025). Obstacle courses are one of the biggest kinds of game on
Roblox, and a jump-and-run game is one of the most common first games children ever play (BCG). The pull is pure skill: you
fail, you try again, and you can feel yourself getting better. Controls that feel natural are one of the strongest reasons
people enjoy a game at all (Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, 2006).

**Core loop.**
1. Pick a course on the world map.
2. The pet runs along by itself. The child taps to jump over gaps and onto platforms.
3. Collect sparkles along the way and hunt for the three stars hidden off the main path.
4. Reach the flag (40 to 90 seconds). See your stars and time. Try again for all three stars, or go to the next course.

**Controls.**
- **One-touch** (the default): the pet runs by itself. Tap to jump. Tap again in the air for a second jump (from world 2). Hold
  to float down slowly.
- **Free move** (for older children): left and right buttons plus a jump button, so you can go back for a missed star.
- Generous timing: a jump still works a moment after you run off an edge, and a tap just before landing counts.

**Progression and how a round ends.**
- Four worlds of six courses (24 in all): Cloud Meadow, Windy Peaks, Starry Night, Rainbow Bridge. Each world adds one new
  thing: springs, moving platforms, wind that pushes you, and switches that open gates.
- Each course has 3 stars to find and a time medal (bronze, silver, gold) to beat your own best.
- Stars unlock the next world and hats and colours for your pet (from the pet wearables we already have).
- A round is a run of courses. After each course the shared end card shows stars, time and the medal, with "Next course" and
  "Map" buttons. Nothing starts by itself.

**What it looks like.** A side view drawn on a canvas: puffy cloud platforms, wooden bridges, springs shaped like flowers, kites,
balloons and a checkered flag, against our sky tokens with two layers of hills and clouds moving at different speeds. The hero is
the child's own pet, drawn once from the pet art into a picture for speed. No enemies to squash: instead there are friendly
puff creatures that bounce you back a little, and wind.

**Sound.** A springy "boing" for a jump (higher for the second jump), sparkle pings that climb the scale as you collect them,
a soft "poof" when you fall, a short fanfare at the flag.

**How it stays healthy.**
- No lives and no game over. Falling puts you back at the last little flag in a second.
- After three falls in the same place, a friendly balloon offers to carry you over. The child can say no.
- 24 courses, then it is finished (time medals give the 10 year olds a reason to replay).
- Medals compare you only with yourself.

**Light learning tie-in.** None. This one is for fun and for skill. (A later idea: a bonus course where the number on each
cloud counts up, so you hop 2, 4, 6, 8. Only if it stays fun.)

**Risks.**
- Jumping games can frustrate a 5 year old: one-touch mode, wide platforms in world 1, the timing help and the balloon helper
  are there for that.
- It must run smoothly on a cheap tablet: a fixed step like Hill Climb's, pictures drawn once and reused, no filters.
- Designing 24 good courses takes time: a short text format for courses keeps it in one chunk; G-04 could ship 12 first and
  12 more later.
- It must not feel like a famous plumber's game: no pipes, mushrooms, coins in blocks or stomping. Clouds, kites and wind make
  it our own.

**Size.** Large. About 45-55 KB including the course data, plus the hub picture. Built on a canvas.

### Game C: Fishing Day

**Pitch:** Take a little boat out for a calm fishing trip: watch the fish swim, drop your line in the right spot, reel them in,
and fill your Fish Book.

**Why children like this kind of game.** Fishing is a quiet favourite in games for all ages, and in late 2025 fishing games were
among the biggest new games on Roblox. It mixes three things children love: collecting (a book with empty spaces), a moment of
skill (the bite and the reel) and surprise (what is that big shadow?). Collecting was at the heart of Google Play's Best Game of
2025. Calm games also help children wind down, one of the ways the UNICEF study (2024) found play can support well-being.

**Core loop.**
1. Pick a fishing place. The screen shows the water from the side, so the child can see the fish swimming below.
2. Tap where to cast. The hook sinks; tap again to stop it at the right depth, near the fish you want.
3. When a fish bites, the float bobs and there is a "plip". Tap to hook it.
4. Hold to reel it in. Each catch shows a card: a drawing of the fish, its name, its size and one short fact (read aloud).
5. Keep it in the bucket or let it go with a splash. Either way it goes in the Fish Book.
6. The trip ends when the bucket holds 8 fish or the sun sets (about 4 to 6 minutes).

**Controls.**
- Tap to cast, tap to stop the hook, tap to hook, hold to reel.
- **Easy reel** (the default for the youngest): holding always brings the fish in.
- **Pro reel** (for older children): a bigger fish pulls back. Hold to reel and let go when the line glows red, so it does
  not snap. If it snaps, the fish just swims off; no loss.

**Progression and how a round ends.**
- Five places, opened by filling the book: the pond, the river, the coral reef, the ice hole and the deep sea.
- About 30 real fish and sea animals to find (trout, catfish, clownfish, pufferfish, salmon, seahorse, octopus ...). Each lives
  in some places and at some depths; some only come in the evening part of the trip; each place has one big rare fish.
- Shells earned on trips buy a new boat colour, a rod, a hat and bait. Better bait helps you find certain fish. All prices are
  shown, and there is nothing random to buy.
- A record board shows the biggest of each fish you caught.
- The end card shows the bucket (each fish drawn), new fish for the book, the biggest catch and stars for the trip.

**What it looks like.** A side view: sky and sun at the top, the boat on the water line, the water in layers getting deeper
and darker blue, weeds and rocks, and drawn fish gliding by. The sun moves across the sky during the trip, so the child can see
time passing without a clock. The fish come from one drawing helper that changes body shape, fins, stripes and colours, in the
same soft style as our fish, crab, shell and turtle pictures.

**Sound.** A plop for the cast, a "plip" for a bite, quick clicks while reeling, a splash and a jingle on a catch, and quiet
water and birds in the background (switched off by the sound button).

**How it stays healthy.**
- The child can see the fish, so catching one is skill and choice, not a slot machine. Chance only decides which fish swim by,
  and if a fish has been missing for a long time it is made to turn up (so nobody gets stuck).
- No "rare fish today only", no daily bonus. The book can be finished.
- Letting fish go is praised. The fish are drawn smiling, and nothing about a catch is scary.

**Light learning tie-in (optional).** Each fish has one true, short fact, read aloud ("A seahorse dad carries the babies"),
which links to Discover. Sizes in centimetres invite "which is bigger?" without asking a question. No quiz.

**Risks.**
- Some families do not like fishing for real animals: catch and release is always offered and praised, and nothing is hurt.
- Calm games can bore action-loving older children: Pro reel and the big rare fish give them a challenge.
- Writing 30 good fish facts takes care: check each one, keep them short and simple.

**Size.** Medium. About 30-40 KB with the fish drawing helper and the fish list, plus the hub picture. Built on a canvas, with
light animation.

## 5. Ranking and recommendation

| Rank | Game | Genre | Who loves it most | Effort | Why this place |
|------|------|-------|-------------------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | **Snack Stand** | Run a shop / pretend play | 5-9, girls and boys | Medium | Fills the biggest gap (no role-play game yet), broad appeal, reuses our drawings, gentle on cheap tablets, natural end of round |
| 2 | **Sky Hop** | Obstacle course / jump-and-run | 7-11, strong with boys | Large | The top genre for this age, the best replay (stars and medals), uses the child's own pet; harder to build and to tune for 5 year olds |
| 3 | **Fishing Day** | Calm collecting | 5-11, everyone | Medium | Calm and lovely, the easiest of the three to get right; less exciting for some older children |

**My recommendation:** build **Snack Stand** first and **Sky Hop** second. Together with Hill Climb, Coloring, Block Builder and
the new racing game (G-02), the Game Room would then have one game of each kind children play most: driving, racing, building,
making art, running a shop and jumping. **Fishing Day** is a good third if Andy wants a calm game for winding down; it would also
be the quickest to build. If only one new game is built, build Snack Stand.

### Ideas I considered and did not pick

- **Endless runner:** very popular, but built on an endless loop. Sky Hop keeps the fun with courses that end.
- **Merge and idle games:** built around waiting and "come back later" timers.
- **Match-3 and block puzzles:** popular, but Brain Teasers already has 17 puzzle types, and the commercial versions lean on
  lives and boosters.
- **Virtual pet:** we already have Pet Care and the Pet Shop.
- **Knock-it-down launcher** (fling a ball at a wobbly tower): children love it and it would fit; it needs a stacking physics
  engine that is risky on cheap tablets. A good fourth idea.
- **Garden defence** (place helpers along a path to keep playful critters off the vegetables): great strategy for 9 to 11 year
  olds, but harder for 5 year olds. A good fifth idea.
- **Music and rhythm:** a nice later idea; needs careful timing on slow devices.
- **Online games** (multiplayer, "io" games): the app has no network, and should not.
